Stage 1 • Level 1.1: The Cable Closet (Cisco NetAcad ITN Deep-Dive)

Welcome to OSI Layer 1: The Physical Layer. To pass the CCNA exam, you must understand how data frames are converted into bits and sent across media formats managed by standards bodies like ISO, IEEE, and TIA/EIA.

1. The 3 Functional Pillars

• Physical Components: Electronic hardware devices, media, and connectors (NICs, ports, patch frames).
• Encoding: Converting data stream bits into recognizable code patterns (e.g., Manchester encoding).
• Signaling: The physical method used to represent a 1 or 0 on the wire (voltage variations, light flashes, or microwave radio pulses).

2. Bandwidth vs. Throughput

• Bandwidth: Theoretical maximum capacity of a physical medium under ideal parameters (measured in bps, Mbps, Gbps).
• Throughput: Actual speed of bits transferred over a specific window of time (reduced by latency and overhead).
• Goodput: The measurement of actual usable application layer data sent after stripping away all header protocol bytes.

3. Copper Media Degradation

• Attenuation: Signals weaken as they travel over long physical distances.
• EMI / RFI: Outward noise signals that distort copper pathways (caused by fluorescent lights or motors).
• Crosstalk: Disturbance caused by magnetic field bleeding from one adjacent wire pair onto another wire pair.

4. Fiber Media Types

• Single-Mode (SMF): Small core, uses expensive lasers to send a single stream of light. Spans massive distances (up to 100km).
• Multimode (MMF): Wider core, uses cheap LEDs to bounce multiple light reflections. Used for localized data centers (up to 550m).
